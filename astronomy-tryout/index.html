Test-day tactics

  • Dump first. The second you get scratch paper, write the equations you know (distance modulus, Hubble's law, Kepler, Tully–Fisher, Cepheid P–L). Now they're safe.
  • Read axes and units before the question. Magnitude axes run backwards (brighter = more negative). Rotation-curve plots: km/s vs kpc. Hubble plots: km/s vs Mpc.
  • If the test gives constants, use theirs.
  • Name the mechanism. Tidal torques, gas dissipation and inflow, density waves, feedback/superwinds, Eddington limit, dark matter halo, r-process nucleosynthesis, orbital decay by gravitational radiation.
  • Identify images by shape: smooth red blob = elliptical; disk+arms = spiral (Sa tight → Sc loose); hat = Sombrero; ring with spokes = Cartwheel; ring + red intruder = Arp 147; two long tails = Antennae; edge-on disk with plumes = M82; dense ball of stars = Terzan 5; no photo at all, just a chirp = GW170817.
  • Period is the fastest discriminator: ~0.5 d = RR Lyrae (old, Pop II, M_V ≈ +0.6); days–weeks = Cepheid (young, Pop I); ~20-day rise + smooth decline = Type Ia.
  • Stuck on a DSO? Write down everything you know anyway — one correct number or one correct mechanism usually earns partial credit.

Scope: what they can ask (2027 rules)

§3.a Content

Stellar classification, spectral features & chemical composition, luminosity, blackbody radiation, spectroscopy, H-R diagrams, Cepheid and RR Lyrae variables, Type Ia supernovas, neutron stars, ultra-luminous X-ray sources (ULXs), gravitational waves; normal & starburst galaxies including morphological classification, galactic structure (stellar populations, globular clusters) and interactions (tidal disruptions, collisions, mergers).

Data may include multi-wavelength and multi-messenger (gravitational waves, neutrinos, cosmic rays) observations, plus proper motions.

§3.b Math

Orbital mechanics, Kepler's laws, rotation & circular motion for binary and galactic systems; parallax, spectroscopic parallax, distance modulus, period–luminosity (Cepheids & RR Lyrae), Tully–Fisher, Type Ia supernovas, Hubble's law; Stefan–Boltzmann and Wien.

National rules let teams bring binders/tablets and 2 Class IV calculators. Your coach is running this tryout closed-notes, so treat the Cheat Sheet as things to memorize or program, not paper to bring.

§3.c The 13 objects

Andromeda Galaxy (M31) · Sombrero Galaxy · M51 · NGC 4536 · MCG+07-33-027 · NGC 1569 · Antennae Galaxies · Arp 143 · Arp 147 · Cartwheel Galaxy · M82 (X-1, X-2, SN 2014J) · GW170817 · Terzan 5.

Grouped the way they'll show up: normal galaxies (M31, Sombrero, M51, NGC 4536) · starbursts (NGC 1569, MCG+07-33-027, M82) · interactions/mergers (Antennae, Arp 143, Arp 147, Cartwheel) · compact objects & multi-messenger (M82 X-1/X-2, GW170817) · globular clusters/stellar populations (Terzan 5).

DSO one-liners

If you remember nothing else

  • Classify by physics, not just looks: Sa→Sc = smaller bulge, looser arms, more gas, bluer, more star formation. Ellipticals = old/red/gas-poor merger products. Irregulars = gas-rich and messy.
  • Pop I = young, metal-rich, disk/arms, Cepheids. Pop II = old, metal-poor, halo/bulge/globular clusters, RR Lyrae.
  • Starburst = a rate: gas used up in ≪ a Hubble time. Triggered by mergers/tides/bars; shows Hα + far-IR + supernovae + ULXs + superwinds.
  • Mergers: stars get rearranged (tidal tails), gas collides and flows inward → starburst + central black-hole growth. Head-on hits make ring galaxies (Cartwheel, Arp 147).
  • Distance rungs: parallax → Cepheids (M_V = −2.76 log P − 1.40) → Type Ia (M_B ≈ −19.3) → Tully–Fisher (L ∝ v_max⁴) → Hubble's law (v = H₀d, H₀ ≈ 70).
  • Rotation curves: flat ⇒ dark matter halo (M(<r) = v²r/G keeps growing). Keplerian fall-off ⇒ mass concentrated at the center.
  • ULX: off-nucleus X-ray source > 10³⁹ erg/s, above the Eddington limit for a stellar-mass black hole → IMBH, super-Eddington accretion, or beaming.
  • GW170817: two neutron stars merged → chirp + short GRB (1.7 s later) + kilonova = gold/platinum via the r-process, plus a standard-siren measurement of H₀.
  • Terzan 5: multiple stellar populations → probably a stripped dwarf-galaxy nucleus, and it holds the record for millisecond pulsars.
