Test-day tactics

  • Dump first. The second you get scratch paper, write the equations you know (parallax, distance modulus, L∝R²T⁴, Wien, Kepler, Δm). Now they're safe.
  • Read axes and units before reading the question. Is the y-axis magnitude (backwards!) or flux? Is x time in days or seconds? Is parallax in mas?
  • If the test gives you constants or a P–L relation, use theirs, not the one you memorized.
  • Trust periods over shapes. ~0.5 d → RR Lyrae; days–weeks → Cepheid; months → Mira; hours → eclipsing binary; ms–s → pulsar.
  • Name the mechanism. Ionization by UV, synchrotron, electron degeneracy, conservation of angular momentum, photoevaporation, radioactive ⁵⁶Ni decay, Doppler shift.
  • For "identify this image" questions: round shell = SNR; ring/bipolar with a hot central star = planetary nebula; pink glow + dark lanes + young stars = star-forming region; long straight streak in radio = magnetic filament.
  • Stuck on a DSO? Write down everything you know about it anyway — partial credit is real, and one correct number often scores.

DSO one-liners

If you remember nothing else

  • OBAFGKM = hot → cool; A stars have the strongest H lines; molecules (TiO) ⇒ M stars.
  • H-R: temperature runs backwards (hot LEFT); white dwarfs bottom-left, giants top-right, instability strip down the middle.
  • L ∝ R²T⁴ and λ_max T = 2.9×10⁶ nm·K.
  • d = 1/p (pc, ″) and m − M = 5 log d − 5.
  • Period tells you the variable: 0.5 d RR Lyrae · 10 d Cepheid · 332 d Mira · hours eclipsing binary · ms pulsar.
  • Chandrasekhar = 1.4 M☉. Type Ia = exploding white dwarf (no H, standard candle, no remnant). Type II = collapsing massive star (H lines, leaves a NS or BH).
  • Emission nebula = ionized gas (red Hα) · reflection = scattered starlight (blue) · dark = dust · synchrotron = radio/X-ray from relativistic electrons.
  • Youth: lithium, disks, IR excess, outflows. Death: white dwarf, neutron star (pulsar), black hole.
