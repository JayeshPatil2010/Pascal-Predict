## Part A — The 13 objects (13 questions)

**A1.** 2.5 million ly away, approaching us at ~110 km/s, tilted disk with two dark dust lanes and two small companions. Hubble found Cepheids here in 1923.

**A2.** ~31 Mly away. Huge white bulge crossed by a razor-thin dark dust ring; looks like a hat. ~2,000 globular clusters.

**A3.** ~23–25 Mly. Face-on two-arm grand-design spiral with a compact companion sitting at the end of one arm. Hosted SN 2011dh (Type IIb).

**A4.** ~50 Mly, an ordinary-looking barred-ish spiral. HST Cepheids were measured here AND it hosted SN 1981B, a Type Ia.

**A5.** ~330 Mly in Hercules. A starburst galaxy with no companion anywhere near it.

**A6.** ~11 Mly, dwarf irregular, two super star clusters, red Hα filaments venting out of the disk.

**A7.** ~45–65 Mly in Corvus. Two merging spirals with two long curved tails of stars and gas.

**A8.** ~190–200 Mly in Lynx. Head-on collision produced a triangular/ring-shaped burst of star formation next to a smooth yellow companion (NGC 2444 + NGC 2445).

**A9.** ~430–440 Mly in Cetus. A blue star-forming ring with nine X-ray-bright black holes in it; ring expanding at ~225 km/s.

**A10.** ~500 Mly in Sculptor. Wagon-wheel: bright outer ring, inner ring, and spokes.

**A11.** ~12 Mly, edge-on starburst with a superwind; hosts ULXs X-1 and X-2 and SN 2014J.

**A12.** Not a galaxy: a ~100-second chirp at 40 Mpc in NGC 4993, then a short gamma-ray burst 1.7 s later, then a kilonova.

**A13.** A bulge cluster ~19,000–22,000 ly away with several stellar populations of different ages and metallicities, and a record number of millisecond pulsars.

**B1.** What makes a galaxy a "starburst" rather than just star-forming?

**B2.** Why do galaxy collisions trigger star formation, when stars essentially never collide?

**B4.** What are tidal tails? What kind of impact makes a ring galaxy?

**B5.** What is a tidal disruption event (TDE)?

**B6.** In a galaxy spectrum, what do **emission** lines tell you? What do **absorption** lines tell you?

**B7.** How do you tell a Cepheid from an RR Lyrae star in one glance at a light curve?

**B8.** Type Ia supernova: what explodes, what is its peak absolute magnitude, and what is left behind?

**B9.** A spiral's rotation curve stays flat far out. What does that imply, and what's the formula behind it?

**B10.** What is a ULX, and what are the three explanations for its luminosity?

**B11.** What is the Eddington limit?

**B12.** GW170817: what was detected, in what order, and why did it matter (give at least two things)?

**B13.** Why do astronomers think Terzan 5 is not a normal globular cluster?

## Part C — The math worth memorizing (7 questions)

**C1.** A star's parallax is 0.02 arcsec. How far away is it?

**C2.** A galaxy has apparent magnitude 15 and absolute magnitude −5. Distance in parsecs?

**C3.** A Cepheid has a period of 10 days. Using M_V = −2.76 log₁₀(P) − 1.40, what is M_V?

**C4.** A galaxy recedes at 7,000 km/s. With H₀ = 70 km/s/Mpc, how far away is it (Mpc)?

**C5.** A spectral line at rest 5000 Å is observed at 5050 Å. What is the recession velocity?

**C6.** A galaxy rotates at 200 km/s at a radius of 8 kpc. Roughly how much mass is enclosed, in solar masses?

**C7.** Two stars orbit with a = 9 AU and P = 3 years. What is their total mass?

# Answer key

**A1.** Andromeda Galaxy (M31) — SA(s)b spiral, mag +3.4, merges with the Milky Way in ~4–5 Gyr.
**A2.** Sombrero Galaxy (M104) — Sa/S0 (debated), SMBH ~10⁹ M☉; the many globular clusters are evidence of an accretion-built halo.
**A3.** M51 (Whirlpool) — SA(s)bc; the interaction with NGC 5195 amplified the arms.
**A4.** NGC 4536 — SAB(rs)bc; it ties two rungs of the distance ladder together (Cepheids + Type Ia).
**A5.** MCG+07-33-027 — the odd one out: a starburst with no trigger.
**A6.** NGC 1569 — IBm dwarf starburst in Camelopardalis (Arp 210), in the IC 342 group.
**A7.** Antennae Galaxies (NGC 4038/4039) — tidal tails; will settle into an elliptical remnant.
**A8.** Arp 143 — head-on collision stripped and compressed NGC 2445's gas into a triangular/ring burst.
**A9.** Arp 147 (IC 298) — collisional ring; the nine X-ray sources are black holes/neutron stars in X-ray binaries left by the burst.
**A10.** Cartwheel Galaxy (ESO 350-40) — "bullseye" collision; the ring is an outward-propagating density wave, full of ULXs.
**A11.** M82 (Cigar Galaxy) — superwind from the starburst; X-1 = IMBH candidate (62-day orbit), X-2 = a ULX *pulsar* (a neutron star), SN 2014J = Type Ia.
**A12.** GW170817 — binary neutron star merger; first GW event with an EM counterpart.
**A13.** Terzan 5 — probably the stripped nucleus of a cannibalized dwarf galaxy (or a bulge building block); 49 known pulsars.

**B1.** The **rate**: it's consuming gas so fast the supply would run out in far less than a Hubble time (MW ~1–3 M☉/yr vs 10–100+ in starbursts). It's a phase, not a galaxy type.
**B2.** The **gas** is dissipative: clouds collide, shock, lose angular momentum and fall inward and compress. Stars are effectively collisionless — they only get rearranged into tails and shells.
**B3.** Pop I = young, metal-rich, disk/spiral arms, Cepheids, blue. Pop II = old, metal-poor, halo/bulge/globular clusters/ellipticals, RR Lyrae, red.
**B4.** Tidal tails are stars+gas flung out by gravitational torques during an encounter. A **head-on "bullseye" collision** through a disk makes a ring galaxy (Cartwheel, Arp 147).
**B5.** A star passing too close to a supermassive black hole is shredded by tidal forces; the fallback of its debris makes a luminous flare.
**B6.** Emission lines (Hα, [O III], [O II]) = ionized gas around hot massive stars = **ongoing star formation**. Absorption lines (Ca II, Mg b, Na D, Balmer) = **starlight from an old/intermediate population**.
**B7.** **Period.** ~0.5 d = RR Lyrae (old Pop II, M_V ≈ +0.6). 1–100 d = Cepheid (young Pop I, sawtooth, brighter).
**B8.** A carbon–oxygen white dwarf detonates near the Chandrasekhar limit (1.4 M☉). Peak M_B ≈ −19.3. Nothing is left — the white dwarf is destroyed.
**B9.** An extended **dark matter halo**: M(<r) = v²r/G keeps growing with radius while the light doesn't. (Keplerian fall-off, v ∝ 1/√r, would mean all the mass is central.)
**B10.** ULX = off-nucleus X-ray source above ~10³⁹ erg/s: (1) intermediate-mass black hole, (2) super-Eddington accretion onto a stellar-mass object, or (3) beamed emission.
**B11.** The luminosity where radiation pressure halts accretion: L_Edd ≈ 1.3 × 10³⁸ (M/M☉) erg/s.
**B12.** LIGO/Virgo saw the chirp; Fermi/INTEGRAL saw the short GRB 1.7 s later; telescopes then found the kilonova. It proved NS mergers make short GRBs and r-process elements (gold, platinum), gave a standard-siren H₀, and showed gravity travels at c.
**B13.** It hosts multiple stellar populations with very different ages and metallicities — normal globular clusters are essentially single-aged and single-metallicity.

**C1.** 50 pc (d = 1/p).
**C2.** 10⁵ pc = 100 kpc (m − M = 20 = 5 log d − 5 → log d = 5).
**C3.** −4.16 (log₁₀ 10 = 1 → −2.76 − 1.40).
**C4.** 100 Mpc (d = v/H₀ = 7000/70) ≈ 326 million ly.
**C5.** ~3,000 km/s (z = 50/5000 = 0.01, v ≈ cz).
**C6.** ~9 × 10¹⁰ M☉ (M ≈ 2.33 × 10⁵ × 200² × 8 ≈ 7.5 × 10¹⁰; any value ~10¹¹ is right).
**C7.** 27 M☉ (M = a³/P² = 729/9).
