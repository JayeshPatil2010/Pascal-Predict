01 · The Problem

Strength testing is slow and expensive

Concrete is the most-used material on Earth after water — but the only way to know a mix's strength is to pour a sample, cure it up to 28 days, and crush it in a hydraulic press. Iterating recipes this way costs weeks per attempt.

THE RESEARCH QUESTION

"Can a simple machine-learning model predict the compressive strength of concrete from its mix ingredients and curing age — accurately enough to screen candidate mixes before physical testing?"

WHY IT MATTERS

Cement production causes ~8% of global CO₂ emissions. A model that finds strong recipes with less cement saves money and carbon — and the same workflow generalizes to alloys, asphalt and 3D-printing materials.

03 · The Method

A five-stage research pipeline

The model is a Random Forest — 60 decision trees embedded in this very page. Each tree learns rules like "IF water is high AND cement is low THEN weak"; the forest averages all opinions so individual mistakes cancel out.

1COLLECT

Load 1,030 samples from the UCI research archive — real recipes, real crush results.

2EXPLORE

Statistics and charts reveal the patterns an engineer would expect — confirming the data is sound.

3LEARN

The forest studies 824 samples (80%): recipe in, measured strength out.

4VERIFY

206 samples (20%) stay hidden during training — the model's final exam.

5APPLY

The verified model powers the Calculator, Mix Designer and Crush Lab below.

09 · Honest Limits & Recommendation

What this model can and cannot do

It interpolates, not extrapolates. The model only understands ingredient ranges present in its training data. Outside them it still outputs a number — a meaningless one.
Lab ≠ field. It predicts lab-cured samples. Real-world weather, compaction and handling add scatter it has never seen.
Screening, not certification. R² = 0.88 is strong, but no structure should be certified from a prediction. The crush test keeps the final word.
Recommendation: pre-screen candidate mixes with the model and physically test only the most promising — saving up to 28 days per rejected recipe and cutting cement wherever a leaner mix still meets spec.
